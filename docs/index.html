Why the bounds matter. Two line equations give a candidate vertex. It belongs to the mapped obstacle only when its x and y coordinates also satisfy both finite segment bounds. Collision checks then compare finite chassis and obstacle edges directly—no occupancy-grid sampling is involved.

Reading the vectors. At each wheel, Fi = Ft,i + Fr,i: the closed-form step splits the command into translational and rotational contributions, then projects the resultant onto that wheel's friction circle. The QP trace repeatedly reduces body-force/moment error while respecting the same per-wheel circles. Under saturation, neither allocation can meet every requested component exactly.

Running blind trajectory. Watch raw wheel odometry diverge on low friction while the observer and closed-form controller keep the chassis clear of obstacles.

How A + B combine in AdvantageScope. Bounded obstacle segments define the collision-free corridor, while the closed-form force allocator keeps per-wheel forces inside dynamic friction circles. When wheels slip on black ice or after an impact, pure wheel odometry (orange) accumulates irreversible drift, whereas the slip-aware observer (blue) and O(1) controller recover the true pose (green).
